Hela Wedakama - Sri Lankan Traditional Medicine

Hela Wedakama is the traditional healing system unique to Sri Lanka. This ancient practice, dating back over 3,000 years, focuses on natural herbal remedies, dietary rules, and special techniques for wellness. It is a heritage of native healing passed down through generations of practitioners.

  • Focuses on natural herbal remedies
  • Blends classical Ayurveda with native wisdom
  • Passed down through generations of healers

The Three Doshas

The central concept of Ayurvedic medicine is the theory that health exists when there is a balance between the three fundamental bodily bio-elements or doshas called Vata, Pitta, and Kapha.

Vata Dosha

Air + Space (Movement)

Characteristics: Dry, cold, light, minute, and movement. All movement in the body is due to properties of vata.

When Unbalanced: Pain, flatulence, gout, rheumatism, and nervous disorders.

Pitta Dosha

Fire + Water (Metabolism)

Characteristics: Heat, moistness, liquidity, sharpness. Represents metabolism and uses bile to direct digestion.

When Unbalanced: Body heat, burning sensation, redness, and inflammatory conditions.

Kapha Dosha

Water + Earth (Structure)

Characteristics: Heaviness, coldness, tenderness, lubrication. The nourishing element that forms soft organs.

When Unbalanced: Congestion, weight gain, sluggishness, and respiratory issues.

Ancient Heritage

History of Hela Wedakama

Ancient Origins - The Era of King Ravana

Some believe the age of "Hela wedakama" comes from the great King Ravana. Regarded as one of the greatest traditional physicians in history, he is credited with writing several ancient medical treatises. Most traditional medicine practitioners still pay respect to King Ravana to obtain blessing prior to starting treatments for patients or making medicines according to ancient traditions.

Royal Physicians - The Era of Great Kings

Sri Lankan kings like Buddhadasa (398 AD) were highly skilled physicians themselves. King Buddhadasa established a network of free hospitals across the country, serving both humans and animals. He wrote the famous medical manuscript "Sarartha Sangrahaya", a comprehensive guide which traditional physicians still use today.

  • First country to establish dedicated hospitals
  • Herbal baths and surgical instruments in ruins
  • Royal patronage for traditional physicians

Historic Village Hospitals

In ancient times, every village had a physician. These practitioners were highly respected members of the community, often working out of village hospital sanctuaries. The Sri Lankan mountain Mihintale still preserves the ancient ruins of what many believe to be the first hospital in the world.

Buddhist Philosophy of Ayurveda

The teachings of the Buddha have deeply influenced traditional Sri Lankan medicine. Compassion for all living beings is the foundation of healing. Mind and body are considered closely interconnected; mental purity and ethical living are crucial to physical health.

  • Mindfulness and mental well-being
  • Vegetarian dietary recommendations
  • Connection between mind and body
  • Ethical living for physical health
