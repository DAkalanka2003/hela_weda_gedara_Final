Ancient Wisdom

Hela Wedakama - Sri Lankan Traditional Medicine

3000 Years of Healing Heritage

Sri Lanka has its own indigenous scheme of traditional medicine called "Hela wedakama". This system has been practiced for many centuries in our island nation. The Sri Lankan Ayurvedic tradition is a unique mixture of Sinhala traditional medicine, Ayurveda and Siddha systems of India, and most importantly, the Desheeya Chikitsa - the indigenous medicine of Sri Lanka.

Bio-Energetic Forces

The Three Doshas

The central concept of Ayurvedic medicine is the theory that health exists when there is a balance between the three fundamental bodily bio-elements or doshas called Vata, Pitta, and Kapha.

Vata Dosha

Air + Space (Movement)

Characteristics: Dry, cold, light, minute, and movement. All movement in the body is due to properties of vata.

When Unbalanced: Pain, flatulence, gout, rheumatism, and nervous disorders.

Pitta Dosha

Fire + Water (Metabolism)

Characteristics: Heat, moistness, liquidity, sharpness. Represents metabolism and uses bile to direct digestion.

When Unbalanced: Body heat, burning sensation, redness, and inflammatory conditions.

Kapha Dosha

Water + Earth (Structure)

Characteristics: Heaviness, coldness, tenderness, lubrication. The nourishing element that forms soft organs.

When Unbalanced: Congestion, weight gain, sluggishness, and respiratory issues.

Ancient Heritage

History of Hela Wedakama

Ancient Origins - The Era of King Ravana

Some believe the age of "Hela wedakama" comes from the great King Ravana, as most traditional medicine practitioners still worship King Ravana to obtain permission prior to starting treatments for patients or making medicines according to ancient traditions.

Royal Physicians and Sacred Manuscripts

King Buddhadasa (398 AD), the most influential of these royal physicians, wrote the Sarartha Sangrahaya, a comprehensive manuscript which Sri Lankan physicians still use today for reference.

World's First Hospitals

Ancient inscriptions on rock surfaces reveal that organized medical services have existed within the country for centuries. The Sri Lankan mountain Mihintale still has the ruins of what many believe to be the first hospital in the world.
